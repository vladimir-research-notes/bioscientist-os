Research memo

Done (M9)

The narrative capstone: one structured memo per research question, assembled from the pipeline with the four epistemic levels kept separate and visible. Not medical advice.

Brief

The memo composes the workbench's existing, transparent outputs for a single research question into one document — without introducing any new, unaudited computation. It re-presents the key papers (BM25F retrieval), the evidence table (Milestone 5), the database links, and the conflicts/uncertainties, and it suggests next experiments only as explicitly unsupported hypotheses. The default output is the Markdown memo itself; --json emits the structured report.

biosci memo \
  --question "What evidence links cellular senescence to chronic inflammation?" \
  --db results/raw/bioscientist.db --out results/processed/memo.md

A research memo is a research-assistance artifact, not medical, diagnostic, or treatment advice. The support/conflict label is a heuristic textual signal, database links are inferred relations, and the suggested experiments are unsupported hypotheses for a human to evaluate — never recommendations (safety).

What the memo contains

Nine sections, each tagged with the epistemic level it belongs to so the four levels are never collapsed:

  1. Research question — the question and its dictionary-recognized entities, with unmatched terms shown honestly (observed metadata).
  2. Corpus and method — counts, the relevance rule, and the named methods used (observed metadata).
  3. Key papers — the papers that contribute evidence, ranked by contribution, with their BM25F retrieval rank shown for reference (observed metadata).
  4. Evidence table — the M5 rows grouped by support label; each row's snippet is an extracted claim, entity names are observed metadata, links are inferred relations, the label is a heuristic textual signal.
  5. Database links — UniProt/HGNC cross-references and Open Targets-style associations for the entities in the evidence (inferred relation).
  6. Conflicts and uncertainties — textual-conflict rows, candidate cross-paper directional disagreements to verify by hand, the unknown-row count, unmatched terms, and the retrieval recall gap.
  7. Suggested next experiments — mechanically generated, question-framed, and explicitly marked unsupported hypothesis / UNTESTED.
  8. Limitations — the honest caveats (tiny corpus, rule-based recall, no cross-paper conflict detection in the per-row label).
  9. Provenance — every evidence row carries paper, exact span, method, and data source; coverage is 100% by construction.

The "suggested next experiments" are the only place the memo reaches beyond the corpus, and they are emitted strictly as status="unsupported" hypotheses framed as research questions ("Whether X …"), never as instructions, protocols, or clinical recommendations (safety).

How it is assembled

The memo introduces no new model: it reuses the Milestone 3 BM25F index (key papers + recall gap), the Milestone 4 dictionary/extraction (claim triples for the candidate-disagreement detector), and the Milestone 5 evidence-table builder (the spine of the memo). Its counts and database links reconcile exactly with the evidence table it is built on (the test suite asserts this). Candidate directional disagreements are found by grouping relation triples of the question's evidence-row claims by their {subject, object} entity pair and flagging pairs where two or more papers assert different relation verbs — a candidate for human review, not an asserted biological conflict.

On the fixtures

One memo is produced per stored research question (assembly is deterministic; memos.json and the Markdown are byte-identical across runs):

Research questionEvidence rows (S / C / U)Candidate disagreementsHypotheses
rq-senescence-inflammation31 (17 / 6 / 8)06
rq-autophagy-senescence29 (15 / 6 / 8)1 (autophagy ↔ cellular senescence: promotes vs. restrains)6
rq-longevity-pathways8 (4 / 1 / 3)04

The designed conflict pair (papers 006 and 007 disagree on the direction of autophagy↔senescence) surfaces as a candidate directional disagreement — the per-row support label cannot detect it (a documented limitation), so the memo flags it as an uncertainty for a human to read and verify. Counts describe memo behavior, not quality or biological truth.
